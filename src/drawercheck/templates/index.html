DrawerCheck

Check a cash drawer against its records

At the end of a shift, a cashier counts the cash in the drawer. DrawerCheck compares that count with the register's records and lists anything worth a second look.

Try it with a made-up month from Demo Store #001: 60 drawers on two registers, with a few records set up to need review.

This free demo server sleeps when nobody is using it. If the page or a check is slow to start, the server is waking up, which usually takes about a minute.

What do these lines mean?
Opening float
Cash put in the drawer at the start of the shift, so the cashier can give change.
Cash sales
Sales paid in cash, as recorded by the register.
Paid-ins
Cash added to the drawer that did not come from a sale.
Cash refunds
Cash given back to customers for returns.
Paid-outs
Cash taken from the drawer to pay for something, such as supplies.
Safe drops
Cash moved from the drawer to the safe during the shift.
Expected cash
What the drawer should hold, worked out from the lines above.
Counted cash
What was actually counted in the drawer at closing.
Difference
Counted cash minus expected cash. Below zero means the drawer is short.
Needs review
The difference is large enough that a person should recount and check the records. It is a lead to check, not a finding about anyone.

    How to prepare your files
    • Keep each file name exactly as listed, and keep the column names in the first row.
    • Write money with two decimal places and no dollar sign or commas, such as 1250.75.
    • Write dates as 2026-09-03 and times as 2026-09-03T08:00:00.
    • Use the same IDs in every file. For example, each shift's cashier_id must also appear in cashiers.csv.
    • Save from your spreadsheet as CSV UTF-8.
